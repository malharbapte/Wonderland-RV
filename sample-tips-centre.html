Travel tips: Q&A in the centre

Desktop only. The questions move to the middle of the band and the photographs frame them. Every tile and the panel sit on one grid, so there is a single gutter everywhere: between photos, and between a photo and the panel.

How the shuffle works, the same in all three
  1. Every 3 seconds one tile crossfades to a new photo. Never the same tile twice in a row.
  2. The new photo is picked at random from the ones that are not on screen and not resting.
  3. Resting: a photo that has just left sits out for the next 8 changes before it can come back.
  4. That rest shortens itself when the pool is small, so something is always free. With no spare photos at all, two tiles simply trade places.
  5. It pauses while the pointer is over the photos, while the tab is hidden, and for anyone who has asked their system to reduce motion.

The grey line under each frame is sample-only: it shows the pool, what is resting, and what is free to come in. To show the rule working it borrows all 23 stills on the site. Live, each page draws only on that family’s own folder.

A: Ring

Ten photos in a frame around a wide panel. Symmetrical and calm; the panel reads like a caption card set into a photo wall.

B: Wings

Four photos either side of a tall centre column. Fewest tiles and the largest photographs; the Q&A becomes the spine of the section.

C: Mosaic

Fourteen photos in mixed shapes (wide, tall and square) around a square panel. The most editorial, and the one that uses a large pool best.
