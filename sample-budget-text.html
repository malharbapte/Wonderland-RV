Budget — the bracket in words

Same four stops, same behaviour, three places the words can live. A stop is a zero-width point with its label absolutely positioned under it, so where thirty-odd characters go is the whole question.

V1 — beside the live value, in the head

The words land where the eye already is. The head is the only line that already changes as you move the handle, so the descriptor arrives with the amount rather than somewhere else on the field. Nothing moves, nothing wraps, and the longest one still fits on the line. This is the one I would ship.

V2 — its own line under the track

More room, and the words get to be a sentence rather than a tail. The cost is a second thing that changes on the field — the head updates and so does this — and a reserved empty line so the disclaimer does not jump when nothing is chosen.

V3 — under every node

All four readable at once, which is the only version where you can compare them before choosing. The cost is real: the scale grows from 0.64 to 1.26rem, the labels need a fixed 2.10rem measure to stop them colliding, and at that width the longest one runs to three lines. Busy, and the field stops looking like a slider.

Two things in your line I had to decide

The bottom bracket moves to $120k. You wrote 120–140; it is $100–140k today. Changing it means the note under the field has to change too, from “under $100,000” to “under $120,000” — which is still true, since Amaroo starts at $127,288. Both are changed here.

The first bracket had no words. Your line gives them to 140–180k (“flexible”) and 180k+ (“willing to invest in the best spec”) but not to 120–140k. I have written “working to a set budget” so the three read in one voice. Change it to whatever you would actually say — it is the one piece of copy here that is mine, not yours.

“Not sure yet” carries no words on purpose. It is not a budget, so giving it a description would make it look like one.
