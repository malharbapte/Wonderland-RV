Option B — seating the quote mark

The layout is option B in all four: lede, film, then the rest of the story. Only the mark moves. What was wrong — its underside sat at exactly 0px from the lede’s cap line and flush to its left edge, so it had no gap in either direction and read as a graphic dropped on the text rather than an opening quote. It was also sitting 3px from the top of the section. Panels are real 375px viewports, scaled to fit four across.

As you saw it

Mark hard against the cap line and hard against the section top. This is the one to fix.

B1 · Above, with air

Smallest change. The mark drops to 42px, gains a 14px gap beneath it, and the section takes 52px at the top so the mark is not pinned to the edge. Same arrangement, just given room to breathe.

B2 · Beside the opening lines — closest to desktop

The mark floats and the lede wraps around it, so it sits to the left of the first words — which is the actual relationship the desktop has, rather than an approximation of it stacked above. Also the most compact of the three.

B3 · On the line

The mark sits inline immediately before the first word, at 30px, the way a typed opening quotation would. Nothing floats above the copy at all — the quietest of the three, and it reads most plainly as punctuation.
