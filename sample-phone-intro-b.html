B1 — putting the mark on the corner

All four are B1: same layout, same calmer spacing, only the mark moves. The desktop puts the mark’s right edge just past the first letter’s left, with its bottom on the cap line. The obstacle on a phone is arithmetic — the copy starts at a 24px gutter, so a 38px mark overlapping the letter by 6px wants to begin at −8px. Each option below solves that a different way. Socials are centred in all of them now. Panels are true 375px viewports, so they wrap to two rows.

B1 as you saw it

Mark stacked above the line with a gap. Correct spacing, but it is not on the letter’s corner — it reads as a separate object sitting over the paragraph.

above the line · no overlap

C1 · Let it hang

Full-size mark on the corner, bleeding about 8px past the gutter into the page margin — which this section can afford, since it runs full bleed anyway. Nothing else moves and the lede keeps its full measure.

on the corner · ~8px into the margin

C2 · Make room for it — exact desktop match

The lede indents 36px so the mark sits wholly on the page. This is the desktop relationship reproduced precisely, nothing off-screen. The cost is 36px of measure on the lede, so it runs a line or two longer.

on the corner · fully on screen · narrower lede

C3 · Shrink it

A 26px mark overlapping by 5px starts at 3px, so it fits the existing gutter untouched: no bleed, no indent, no lost measure. The mark reads more as punctuation than as a graphic, which may or may not be what you want it doing.

on the corner · fits as-is · smaller mark
