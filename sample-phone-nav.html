The phone menu

Three ways to handle the depth. Each frame is a true 375px viewport with the menu already open.

What it does now: the header dumps every link out flat — all 15, the six top-level items plus the entire About mega-menu including all six Wonderlander names. That is 502px of centred list. Two other faults come along with it, and all three options fix them because they are faults rather than choices: the rows are 39px where a tap target wants 44, and “Build your Caravan” is display:none on the phone — the site’s primary action is the one thing the menu will not offer. The list also left-aligns in all three: centred text starts at a different x on every line, which is what makes a menu hard to run your eye down.

A — Accordion

Six rows until you ask for more. About carries a chevron and drops its nine children beneath it, on the same open-and-close the Wonderlander cards and the van-layout headings already use. The desktop menu’s structure survives intact — it is just folded up. Everything stays on one screen. 405px closed, 748 open.

B — Drill-down

One screen at a time. Six rows, and About slides the panel across to its own screen with a back row at the top. Nothing is ever longer than seven rows, and the level you are on is always named. The pattern every phone app already teaches. 400px on level one, 573 on level two.

C — Fewer links

No interaction to learn — a content decision instead. The six Wonderlander names come out of the menu: they are one tap away on the Wonderlanders page, which is where they live and where they have their photographs. Six primary rows, then the three About destinations as a quieter group. Nine rows plus the CTA, nothing hidden. The win here is fewer links, not a shorter panel: 546px.

Tap About in A and B. Desktop is untouched in all three — every rule sits inside the phone block, and the header above 901px keeps its mega-menu exactly as it is.
